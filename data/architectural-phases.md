# 600 Stockton Street — Architectural Phase Diagram

## Purpose

This file documents the physical evolution of the Metropolitan Life
Insurance Company building at 600 Stockton Street.

The diagrams are conceptual spatial representations based on currently
available historical evidence. They are not architectural drawings and
should not be interpreted as dimensionally accurate until dimensions have
been verified through primary sources.

---

# Phase 1 — Original Metropolitan Life Building

## 1908–1909

The original Metropolitan Life building was constructed as the company's
Pacific Coast Head Office.

**Architects:** Napoleon LeBrun & Sons; James Rupert Miller

**Style:** Classical Revival / Neoclassical

**Construction:** Steel and reinforced concrete

### Original Geometry

PCAD describes the original portion of the building as a rectangle with
the long side running approximately 80 feet along Stockton Street.

### Stockton Street Facade

The original facade was organized into a five-bay composition:

- two end bays
- two connecting bays
- central pediment
- engaged Ionic columns
- prominent pedimented portico

The exterior was clad in white terra cotta.

# Phase 2 — First Major Expansion

## 1913–1914

Two wings were added to the original building, extending toward
California Street and Pine Street.

The resulting configuration became substantially larger and responded
to the steep Nob Hill site.

The expansion is described as including:

- additional offices
- rooftop garden
- dining room
- kitchen
- subbasement

# Phase 3 — Construction Evidence

## May 12, 1915

OpenSFHistory photograph:

**Pine & Stockton**

**Image ID:** `wnp36.00789`

**Photographer:** Horace Chaffee

**Collection:** San Francisco Department of Public Works

The photograph documents the Metropolitan Life Insurance building under
construction at Pine and Stockton Streets.

### Why This Photograph Matters

This is direct visual evidence of construction activity at the building
in 1915.

It should be compared against:

- the 1913–1915 Sanborn map
- the 1919 Sanborn map
- the PCAD architectural record
- other 1914–1915 photographs

### Reconstruction Question

The photograph may help distinguish portions of the building associated
with the original 1908–1909 structure from portions associated with the
later expansion.

However, the photograph alone does not establish the construction date
or architect of individual architectural elements.

**Evidence status:** Direct photographic evidence.

**Source:** OpenSFHistory, `wnp36.00789`

# Important Chronology Note

Different authoritative sources currently provide slightly different
dates and descriptions for some expansion phases.

San Francisco Planning documentation identifies:

- 1914 — two symmetrical wings
- 1920–22 — major expansion
- 1930 — Pine Street wing
- 1954 — California Street wing and garden court

PCAD identifies:

- 1913–14 — two wings extending toward California and Pine Streets
- 1919–20 — additional major office expansion

These differences may reflect differences between construction,
completion, alteration, and documentation dates.

The project will preserve these discrepancies rather than silently
choosing one chronology.

Further research will use Sanborn maps, building permits, architectural
drawings, photographs, and contemporary sources to resolve the timeline.

---

# Evidence Classification

The project uses four evidence levels:

### A — Direct Evidence

A photograph, map, drawing, document, or other primary source directly
shows the feature or event.

### B — Strong Documentary Evidence

An authoritative architectural, historical, or preservation source
documents the feature or event.

### C — Reasonable Inference

The feature is not directly documented but is supported by multiple
independent pieces of evidence.

### D — Speculative

The feature is reconstructed primarily from assumptions or analogy.

Speculative elements will not be presented to visitors as established
historical fact.

# Reconstruction Principle

The ultimate digital reconstruction will represent 600 Stockton Street
as a building that evolved over time rather than as a single static
structure.

The eventual interactive experience should allow users to explore the
building through its major architectural phases:

**1909 → 1914 → 1915 → 1920 → 1930 → 1954 → 1990 → Today**

Each phase will be connected to its underlying historical evidence.
