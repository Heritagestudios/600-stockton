# 600 Stockton Street — Site Evolution

## Purpose

This dataset tracks documented physical and functional changes at the
600 Stockton Street site using primary historical maps and other
verified sources.

## Timeline

| Date | Block | Primary occupant/use | Physical evidence | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1887 | 116 | Grace Church Episcopal | Large church structure; associated garden and Grace Terrace | 1887 Sanborn Map |
| 1913 | 257 (116) | Metropolitan Life Insurance Co. | Fireproof concrete and steel-frame building on Stockton side of block | 1913–1915 Sanborn Map |
| 1919 | 257 (116) | Metropolitan Life Insurance Co. | More complex/expanded building configuration; Metropolitan Life building remains identified on Stockton | 1919 Sanborn Map |

## Key Observations

### 1887
Grace Church Episcopal is the dominant structure on the site. The
Sanborn map also identifies a garden and Grace Terrace.

### 1913
The former Grace Church site is represented as Block 257 (116).
The principal building is identified as the Metropolitan Life Insurance
Company's building. The map identifies fireproof concrete and steel-frame
construction.

### 1919
The Metropolitan Life building remains the principal structure. The
map shows a more complex building configuration than the 1913 map,
including additional building mass toward the interior of the block.

## Research Questions

1. When exactly did Grace Church leave the site?
2. When was the Metropolitan Life building constructed?
3. What portions of the 1913 building correspond to the original 1909 structure?
4. What additions were made between 1913 and 1919?
5. How does the Sanborn evidence correspond to the 1914 Metropolitan Life additions documented by San Francisco's historic resource records?
6. How did the building's relationship to Stockton Street change over time?
7. What happened to the Grace Church garden and terrace after 1906?

## Evidence Standard

Sanborn maps are treated as primary historical evidence. Interpretations
should be distinguished from observations directly visible on the maps.
## Building Footprint Evolution

The physical history of 600 Stockton can be understood as a sequence of
building expansions rather than a single construction event.

### 1886–1887 — Pre-Metropolitan Life Site

The 1886–1887 Sanborn map documents the condition of the site before the
Metropolitan Life headquarters was constructed.

This establishes the baseline for understanding what was replaced by the
1909 building.

**Evidence:** Sanborn Fire Insurance Map, 1886–1887.

**Evidence level:** Primary source.

---

### 1909 — Original Metropolitan Life Headquarters

The original Metropolitan Life building was completed in 1909.

The earliest structure occupied the Stockton-facing portion of the site
and was substantially smaller than the later complex.

The building was a steel-frame office structure designed by
Napoleon LeBrun & Sons, with James Rupert Miller associated with the
San Francisco work.

The original footprint is currently being reconstructed as a rectangular
form.

There is a dimensional discrepancy between sources:

- Sanborn evidence currently suggests approximately 60 feet of Stockton
  frontage and approximately 80 feet of depth.
- PCAD describes approximately 80 feet of Stockton frontage.

This discrepancy remains unresolved and is intentionally preserved in the
research dataset rather than being reconciled without additional evidence.

**Evidence level:** Mixed primary and secondary sources.

---

### 1913–1915 — First Expansion Period

The 1913–1915 Sanborn map documents the building during an early expansion
period.

This map is particularly important because it allows the original 1909
building to be compared with the enlarged structure.

Photographs from approximately 1914–1915 provide complementary visual
evidence for the exterior appearance during this period.

The footprint will be digitized directly from the Sanborn map and then
compared with the photographic record.

**Evidence:** Sanborn Fire Insurance Map, 1913–1915; 1914–1915 photographs.

**Evidence level:** Primary visual and cartographic sources.

---

### 1919 — Expanded Metropolitan Life Complex

The 1919 Sanborn map documents a substantially larger building complex.

The footprint contains multiple building sections and an interior open
space/courtyard.

The 1919 map is therefore a key transition point between the original
1909 headquarters and the mature multi-wing complex.

The exact relationship between the original core and individual later
additions remains an active research question.

**Evidence:** Sanborn Fire Insurance Map, 1919, Sheet 47.

**Evidence level:** Primary source.

---

### 1953 — Mature Historic Complex

By 1953 the building had undergone multiple expansion campaigns and had
developed into the large multi-wing complex that forms the basis of the
present building.

The courtyard and surrounding wings represent the accumulated result of
these construction phases.

This period will serve as the later historic footprint against which the
1909 and 1919 structures can be compared.

**Evidence level:** Secondary architectural documentation.

---

## Reconstruction Principle

The project will distinguish between:

1. **Documented geometry** — directly visible or measurable in historic
   maps and plans.

2. **Documented architectural features** — supported by photographs,
   architectural descriptions, or other primary sources.

3. **Interpretive reconstruction** — historically informed elements that
   are not directly documented.

4. **Unknowns** — features for which evidence has not yet been located.

No uncertain dimension or architectural feature should be presented as
fact without identifying its evidentiary status.
