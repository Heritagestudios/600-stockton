### Significance

This photograph is particularly important because it documents the
Metropolitan Life Insurance building during construction.

The photograph provides visual evidence for the physical state of the
building in 1915 and may help distinguish the original building from
later additions.

The image should be compared with:

- the 1913–1915 Sanborn map
- the 1919 Sanborn map
- other 1914–1915 photographs
- the San Francisco historic-resource record

### Research Questions

1. Which portions of the building visible in the photograph correspond
   to the original 1909 structure?
2. Which portions are under construction in 1915?
3. Can the photograph identify the 1914 additions described in the
   historic-resource record?
4. Can the construction sequence be matched to the Sanborn footprint?
5. What architectural details are visible before completion?
6. What does the construction process reveal about the building's
   steel/concrete structure?

### Evidence Status

**Direct photographic evidence.**

The photograph directly documents construction activity at the
Metropolitan Life Insurance building in May 1915.

The photograph does not, by itself, establish the date or architect
of individual portions of the construction.

**Citation:** OpenSFHistory / wnp36.00789

## Site Identification

The map identifies the property as Block 257 (116) and labels the
principal building:

**Metropolitan Life Insurance Co's Bldg.**

## Physical Observations

- The Metropolitan Life building occupies the Stockton-facing portion
  of the block.
- The building configuration is more complex than the earlier 1913
  representation.
- Additional building mass extends into the interior portion of the block.
- Interior/courtyard space is visible.
- The map includes handwritten field/revision annotations.
- The second page contains a handwritten index referring to Block 257
  (116) and neighboring blocks.

## Construction

The map's building notation should be read together with the 1913
Sanborn evidence documenting fireproof concrete and steel-frame
construction.

## Interpretation

The 1919 map provides evidence that the Metropolitan Life complex had
developed beyond the simpler configuration represented in the 1913 map.

This observation should be investigated against architectural and
building-permit records before assigning specific dates or architects
to individual additions.

## New Architectural Evidence — Pacific Coast Architecture Database

The Pacific Coast Architecture Database (PCAD) provides additional
architectural information about the Metropolitan Life Insurance Company
Office Building at 600 Stockton Street.

PCAD dates construction to 1908–1909 and identifies the building as
San Francisco Historic Landmark No. 167.

### Original Building Geometry

PCAD describes the original portion of the building as a rectangle,
with its long side paralleling approximately 80 feet of Stockton Street.

The original Stockton Street facade consisted of a five-bay composition:

- central pediment
- two end bays
- two connecting bays
- engaged Ionic columns
- prominent pedimented portico

This provides important evidence for the reconstruction of the original
1909 facade.

### Architectural Attribution

PCAD identifies Napoleon LeBrun and Sons as the architectural firm.

The record identifies Pierre L. Le Brun and Michel Moracin Le Brun as
working on the San Francisco commission with local architect James Rupert
Miller.

Further research is required to establish the precise division of
responsibility between LeBrun & Sons and Miller.

### 1913–1914 Expansion

PCAD states that James Rupert Miller's firm supervised the construction
of two wings extending down California Street and Pine Street.

The addition created a U-shaped configuration stepping down the steep
site.

PCAD identifies additional offices, a rooftop garden, and dining room
as part of this phase.

### 1919–1920 Expansion

PCAD identifies another major office addition in 1919–1920, also handled
by Miller's firm.

A young Timothy Pflueger supervised the work.

### Chronology Discrepancy

San Francisco Planning documentation and PCAD provide slightly different
dates for the expansion phases.

San Francisco Planning:
- 1914 — two symmetrical wings
- 1920–22 — major expansion

PCAD:
- 1913–14 — two wings along California and Pine Streets
- 1919–20 — additional office expansion

The discrepancy will be investigated through primary sources.

Neither chronology should be treated as definitive until the underlying
documentation has been examined.
