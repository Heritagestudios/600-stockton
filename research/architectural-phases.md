# 600 Stockton Street — Architectural Phases

## Purpose

This file documents the physical evolution of the Metropolitan Life
Insurance Company building at 600 Stockton Street.

The objective is to reconstruct the building as a sequence of
architectural phases rather than treating the present building as a
single construction event.

---

# Phase 1 — Original Metropolitan Life Building

## 1908–1909

**Architects:** Napoleon LeBrun & Sons; James Rupert Miller

**Original use:** Metropolitan Life Insurance Company Pacific Coast
Head Office

**Style:** Classical Revival / Neoclassical

**Construction:** Steel and reinforced concrete

### Original Geometry

PCAD describes the original portion as a rectangle with the long side
running approximately 80 feet along Stockton Street.

### Stockton Street Facade

The original facade was organized into five bays:

- two end bays
- two connecting bays
- central pediment
- engaged Ionic columns
- prominent pedimented portico

The architecture communicated institutional permanence, authority and
wealth through a monumental Classical vocabulary.

### Reconstruction Status

**Evidence level: B — Strong documentary evidence**

The original geometry and facade organization are documented by PCAD.

Exact dimensions, window proportions, interior organization and
decorative details require additional primary-source evidence.

---

# Phase 2 — First Expansion

## 1913–1914

**Architect:** James Rupert Miller's firm

### Physical Change

Two wings were added:

- California Street wing
- Pine Street wing

Each wing extended approximately 28 feet from the original structure.

The resulting building formed a U-shaped configuration.

The building stepped down the steep Nob Hill site.

### Program

The expansion added:

- offices
- rooftop garden
- dining room
- kitchen
- subbasement

### Context

The expansion occurred concurrently with construction of the Stockton
Street Tunnel, completed December 29, 1914.

### Reconstruction Status

**Evidence level: B — Strong documentary evidence**

Further comparison with Sanborn maps and photographs is required to
establish the exact geometry and construction sequence.

# Phase 3 — Construction Evidence

## 1915

### May 12, 1915

OpenSFHistory photograph:

**Pine & Stockton**

**Image ID:** wnp36.00789

The photograph documents the Metropolitan Life Insurance building under
construction on Stockton Street between Pine and California Streets.

**Photographer:** DPW photographer Horace Chaffee

### Significance

This photograph provides direct visual evidence of construction activity
at the building in 1915.

It should be compared with:

- 1913–1915 Sanborn map
- 1919 Sanborn map
- PCAD description
- later photographs

### Reconstruction Status

**Evidence level: A — Direct photographic evidence**

The image directly documents construction conditions in May 1915.

The photograph alone does not establish which individual architectural
elements belong to the 1909 structure versus the 1913–14 expansion.

---

# Phase 4 — Major Expansion

## 1919–1920

**Architect:** James Rupert Miller's firm

**Supervising architect:** Timothy Pflueger

### Physical Change

A major additional expansion increased the building's office capacity.

PCAD describes this as another large addition of offices.

### Architectural Development

The later work increasingly transformed the original Metropolitan Life
building into a larger unified complex.

### Reconstruction Status

**Evidence level: B — Strong documentary evidence**

Exact geometry requires comparison with the 1919 Sanborn map,
photographs and architectural documentation.

# Reconstruction Questions

## Phase 1

- Can the original 1909 footprint be established precisely?
- Can the five-bay facade be measured from photographs?
- Can original window dimensions be established?
- Can original roof height be established?
- Can the original portico be reconstructed?

## Phase 2

- Which portions of the 1915 photograph represent the original building?
- Which portions represent the 1913–14 wings?
- Can the 1913–15 Sanborn map establish the footprint?
- Can the 1919 map establish the completed configuration?

## Phase 3

- What changed between 1915 and 1919?
- Did the later expansion alter the Stockton facade?
- When was the monumental pediment/statue group added?
- Can architectural drawings identify the transition?

# Evidence Rules

The reconstruction will distinguish:

**A — Direct evidence**

Photographs, maps, drawings or other primary sources directly showing
the feature.

**B — Strong documentary evidence**

Authoritative architectural or preservation records.

**C — Reasonable inference**

Interpretation supported by multiple independent sources.

**D — Speculative**

A reconstruction assumption not directly supported by available
evidence.

Speculative elements will not be presented to visitors as historical
fact.
