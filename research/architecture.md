# 600 Stockton Street — Architectural Evolution

## Project Question

How did the architecture of 600 Stockton Street evolve from its construction
in 1909 through its later expansions and conversion into a hotel?

This research separates documented architectural evidence from interpretation
and will eventually support a digital/immersive reconstruction of the site.

---

## 1. Original Building — 1909

**Historic Name:** Metropolitan Life — Pacific Coast Head Office  
**Year Built:** 1909  
**Architect:** Lebrun and Sons  
**Style:** Classical Revival  
**Construction:** Steel and reinforced concrete  
**Exterior:** White terra cotta  
**Stories:** 4–7B  
**Original Owner:** Metropolitan Life Insurance Company  
**Original Tenant:** Metropolitan Life Insurance Company  
**Original Use:** Educational facility [historic-resource record]  
**Current Use:** Luxury hotel  

### Architectural Description

The historic-resource record describes the original structure as a massive
Classical Revival building occupying a prominent portion of the block bounded
by Stockton, California, Pine, and Grant Avenue.

The original building was described as a nearly cubic classical temple in
the Ionic order, with substantial interior space.

The principal architectural value is identified as the monumental Stockton
Street facade, particularly its Ionic portico and the heroically scaled
statuary group executed by Haig Patigian.

### Site Context

The site had previously been occupied by Grace Church, which was destroyed
during the 1906 San Francisco fire.

The selection and development of the site therefore represent a major
architectural transition:

**Grace Church / Gothic Revival**
→ **Metropolitan Life / Classical Revival**

### Primary Research Questions

- Why did Metropolitan Life select this site after the 1906 disaster?
- Why was Classical Revival chosen for the company's Pacific Coast
  headquarters?
- What did the original 1909 building look like before later additions?
- How did the Ionic portico communicate Metropolitan Life's institutional
  identity?
- What was the original relationship between the building and the steep
  Nob Hill site?
- What evidence survives for the original interior?

## 2. Documented Additions and Alterations

The historic-resource record identifies several major phases of expansion.

### 1914 — Two Symmetrical Wings

Two symmetrical wings were added to the original building, probably by
Lebrun.

**Research question:**  
Can the 1913–1915 Sanborn map help identify these additions and establish
their physical relationship to the original 1909 structure?

### 1920–1922 — Major Expansion

Miller & Colmesnil undertook a major expansion and alteration that included
the subdivision of the central space.

**Research questions:**

- What portion of the building was altered?
- What did "subdivision of the central space" mean architecturally?
- Can Sanborn maps, permits, or architectural drawings identify the changes?

### 1930 — Pine Street Wing

Miller & Pfleuger added the Pine Street wing.

**Research question:**  
How did this addition change the building's relationship to Pine Street and
the overall block?

### 1954 — California Street Wing and Garden Court

Thomsen & Wilson added the California Street wing and a garden court.

**Research questions:**

- What did the garden court look like?
- How did this addition connect the building to California Street?
- Did the intervention alter the original 1909 composition?

### 1990 — Hotel Conversion

The building underwent a major rehabilitation and conversion into a
Ritz-Carlton hotel by Whisler-Patri.

**Research questions:**

- Which historic architectural elements were retained?
- Which spaces were reconstructed or adapted?
- How did the hotel conversion affect the historic interior?
- Which elements visible to a hotel guest today can be traced to 1909?

## 4. Evidence We Currently Have

### Sanborn Maps

The Sanborn maps provide a way to compare the physical footprint of the
property across time:

- 1886–1887
- 1913–1915
- 1919

The 1913–1915 map identifies the Metropolitan Life building and documents
steel-frame/fireproof-concrete construction.

The 1919 map shows the Metropolitan Life building with a more complex
configuration than the earlier map.

These maps will be compared against the documented architectural phases
above.

### Historic Preservation Record

San Francisco Planning identifies the property as:

**A — Historic Resource Present**

and identifies the building as:

**Met Life — Pacific Coast Head Office**

with:

**Landmark No. 167**

The property is also identified as an **Article 11 Category I —
Significant Building, No Alterations**.

## 5. Evidence vs. Interpretation

This project will distinguish between:

**DOCUMENTED**
Information explicitly supported by a historical source.

**OBSERVED**
Something directly visible in a map, photograph, drawing, or other
primary source.

**INTERPRETED**
Our analysis of what the evidence may mean.

No architectural phase will be assigned to a specific portion of the
building solely from visual similarity. Dates and authorship will be
verified against primary or authoritative records where possible.
