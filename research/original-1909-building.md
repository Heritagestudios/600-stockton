# Original 1909 Metropolitan Life Building

## Research Question

What did the original Metropolitan Life Pacific Coast Head Office
designed by Lebrun and Sons look like in 1909, before the later
additions and alterations?

The goal is to distinguish the original 1909 structure from later
architectural interventions and eventually create a historically
grounded digital reconstruction.

---

## 1. What We Know

### Building

**Historic name:** Metropolitan Life — Pacific Coast Head Office

**Address:** 600 Stockton Street, San Francisco

**Year built:** 1909

**Architect:** Lebrun and Sons

**Architectural style:** Classical Revival

**Construction:** Steel and reinforced concrete

**Exterior material:** White terra cotta

**Historic resource classification:** A — Historic Resource Present

**Landmark:** San Francisco Landmark No. 167

---

## 2. Architectural Character

San Francisco's historic-resource documentation describes the original
building as a nearly cubic Classical Revival temple in the Ionic order.

The principal architectural significance is associated with the
monumental Stockton Street facade.

Important documented elements include:

- Ionic portico
- Monumental scale
- Classical Revival composition
- White terra-cotta exterior
- Heroically scaled statuary group
- Statuary attributed to Haig Patigian
- Substantial interior space

---

## 3. Original Site

The site was previously occupied by Grace Church, which was destroyed
during the 1906 earthquake and fire.

The 1886–1887 Sanborn map documents Grace Church Episcopal on the site.

The 1913–1915 Sanborn map documents the Metropolitan Life building.

This provides evidence for a major transformation of the site between
the two map periods:

**Grace Church**
→ **1906 destruction**
→ **Metropolitan Life headquarters**

## 5. 1913–1915 as a Baseline

The 1913–1915 Sanborn map identifies the Metropolitan Life Insurance
Company's building and documents fireproof construction using steel
and concrete.

Because this map postdates the 1909 construction, it should be treated
as evidence of the building's configuration by 1913–1915 rather than
automatically as a perfect representation of the original 1909 building.

## 8. Reconstruction Evidence

The eventual digital reconstruction should only model elements for which
we have sufficient historical evidence.

Each reconstructed element should eventually receive an evidence level:

**A — Direct evidence**  
Visible in an original photograph, drawing, map, or other primary source.

**B — Strong documentary evidence**  
Supported by authoritative architectural or preservation documentation.

**C — Reasonable inference**  
Not directly documented but strongly supported by surrounding evidence.

**D — Speculative**  
Included only to communicate an interpretive possibility.

Speculative elements should never be presented to visitors as historical
fact.

3. Were the Ionic portico and statuary completed in 1909?

    with each architectural phase represented separately?
