# 600 Stockton Street — Primary Source Register

## Purpose

This register identifies the primary and high-value secondary sources being
used to reconstruct the history and architecture of 600 Stockton Street.

Sources are organized by the historical question they help answer.

---

# 1. Sanborn Fire Insurance Maps

## 1886–1887

**Source:** Sanborn Fire Insurance Map, 1886–1887

**Research purpose:**

Establish the physical condition of the site before construction of the
Metropolitan Life headquarters.

**Primary questions:**

- What occupied the site?
- What were the lot boundaries?
- What structures existed?
- How did the site relate to California, Stockton, Pine, and Grant?

**Use in reconstruction:**

Historical site baseline.

**Status:**

Available in project research collection.

---

## 1913–1915

**Source:** Sanborn Fire Insurance Map, 1913–1915

**Research purpose:**

Document the Metropolitan Life building during the early expansion period.

**Primary questions:**

- What was the building footprint?
- How large was the original structure?
- What additions had occurred?
- What materials and construction types are identified?
- What portions of the building were occupied?

**Use in reconstruction:**

Primary footprint evidence for the early expansion period.

**Status:**

Available in project research collection.

---

## 1919

**Source:** Sanborn Fire Insurance Map, 1919, Sheet 47

**Research purpose:**

Document the substantially expanded Metropolitan Life complex.

**Primary questions:**

- What was the complete footprint?
- Where was the interior courtyard/open space?
- Which wings existed?
- What construction materials are identified?
- What portions appear to be additions?

**Use in reconstruction:**

Primary footprint evidence for the 1919 phase.

**Status:**

Available in project research collection.

evidence.

maps or original architectural documents.

# 7. Architectural Materials

## Gladding, McBean & Company

**Research purpose:**

Investigate original terra-cotta production and architectural fabrication.

**Primary questions:**

- Did Gladding, McBean manufacture terra-cotta for the Metropolitan Life
  building?
- Which construction phase did the work belong to?
- Do project drawings survive?
- Are shop drawings available?
- Are molds, specifications, or correspondence preserved?
- Can individual architectural elements be attributed to specific
  construction phases?

**Potential reconstruction value:**

Very high.

# 8. Original Architectural Documentation

The project should continue searching for:

- original architectural drawings
- structural drawings
- floor plans
- elevations
- sections
- specifications
- contracts
- building permits
- alteration permits
- construction photographs

These sources have the highest potential value for resolving the
remaining geometry questions.

# 9. Grace Church / Pre-1909 Site

The site history must also include the structure that occupied the
location before Metropolitan Life.

**Building:**

Grace Church

**Location:**

Southeast corner of California and Stockton Streets.

**Use in project:**

Establish the historical transition:

Grace Church → 1906 destruction → redevelopment → Metropolitan Life.

This should eventually become a separate historical layer in the
immersive experience.

---

# Source Priority

For physical reconstruction, prioritize:

1. Original architectural drawings
2. Building permits
3. Sanborn maps
4. Historic photographs
5. Contemporary newspaper documentation
6. Historic surveys
7. Landmark documentation
8. Architectural databases
9. Later secondary sources

---
